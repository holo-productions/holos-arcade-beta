Rules — Chess

The full rulebook: endings, special moves, scoring

The goal

Checkmate the enemy king: attack it so that no legal move — moving the king, capturing the attacker, or blocking the attack — can save it. The side that delivers checkmate wins. You play White and always move first.

How a game can end

  • Checkmate — a king in check with no escape loses.
  • Resignation — either side may resign at any time and loses.
  • Stalemate (draw) — the side to move is not in check but has no legal move.
  • Threefold repetition (draw) — the exact same position, with the same side to move, occurs three times.
  • Fifty-move rule (draw) — 50 moves by each side pass with no pawn move and no capture.
  • Insufficient material (draw) — neither side can possibly checkmate: king vs king, king + bishop or knight vs king, or king + bishop vs king + bishop with bishops on the same colour.

Check

A king under attack is in check and must be gotten out of it on that move. You may never make a move that leaves your own king in check — the board simply won't offer it. A move that gives check is written with a "+", checkmate with a "#".

Special moves

  • Castling — once per game per side, the king moves two squares toward a rook and the rook hops over it. Legal only if neither piece has moved, the squares between them are empty, the king is not in check, and the king does not pass through or land on an attacked square.
  • En passant — if an enemy pawn advances two squares and lands beside your pawn, you may capture it as if it had moved only one square. Only on the very next move — after that the chance is gone.
  • Promotion — a pawn reaching the far rank must become a queen, rook, bishop, or knight of the same colour (your choice, and yes, you can end up with two queens).

Scoring — piece values

Chess itself has no points, but players judge who is ahead by material. These are the values this game uses for the "up in material" counter, and roughly how strong each piece is:

House notes for this version

  • Tap a piece, then tap a highlighted square — dots are quiet moves, rings are captures. No touch-move rule: nothing counts until you tap the destination.
  • Undo takes back your last move and the AI's reply. Changing difficulty starts a fresh game and doesn't count the abandoned one.
  • Practice round — tap 🎯 Practice round for an untimed, unscored game: hints are available, undo works anytime, and the result never touches your record.
  • Pip (Easy) thinks one move ahead and daydreams; Nova (Normal) thinks two; Vex (Hard) thinks three, with extra time spent checking captures.
