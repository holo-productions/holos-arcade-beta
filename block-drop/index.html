How to play

Pieces made of four blocks fall from the top of a 10 × 20 well. Move and spin each piece as it falls so it lands filling complete horizontal rows. A full row disappears — that's how you score and keep the well from filling up.

Your goal

Survive as long as you can and score as much as you can. Clearing four rows at once (a “Block Drop”) is worth the most. As you clear lines you level up — and the pieces fall faster.

Try a practice round first

Tap 🎯 Practice round (in the Mode panel, on the start screen, or the amber button under Start) to warm up with no pressure. Practice is untimed — pieces don't fall on their own, they wait until you press ↓ or Space. There's no score and no best to beat, and hints stay on: an amber outline on the board shows a suggested landing spot, with a written hint under the board (where to move, how many spins, and whether it clears a line). Use it to learn the pieces, test a hold swap, or just stack for fun. Switch back to Scored game whenever you're ready — your scored bests are untouched by practice.

Pick a difficulty

Choose Easy, Normal, or Hard in the Difficulty panel (or on the start screen) before you play. Easy falls slower and levels up every 12 lines, Normal is the classic pace at every 10 lines, and Hard starts fast and levels up every 8 lines. Changing difficulty mid-game takes effect when you restart, and each difficulty keeps its own best score. Difficulty doesn't change Practice — Practice is always untimed.

Rules

The well & the pieces

  • The well is 10 columns wide and 20 rows tall.
  • Pieces are drawn from a shuffled bag of all seven shapes, so every piece appears once before the bag repeats — no endless droughts or floods of one shape.
  • A piece locks into place when it lands and can't fall any further.

Scoring

Lines cleared at onceBase points
1 — Single100
2 — Double300
3 — Triple500
4 — Block Drop!800
  • Line points are multiplied by your current level (e.g. a Single at level 3 = 300 points).
  • Soft drop: +1 point per cell. Hard drop: +2 points per cell.
  • Clearing lines on consecutive pieces builds a combo: +50 × combo count × level.
  • Back-to-back four-line clears earn a 50% bonus on the second and later ones in a row.

  • Difficulty is picked before a game starts. Changing it mid-game applies to the next game (or right away if you tap Restart).
  • Each difficulty keeps its own best score on this device. Scores earned before difficulty settings existed count as Normal bests.
  • Everything else — pieces, scoring, hold, combos — works the same on every difficulty. Only the fall speed and how quickly you level up change.

Levels & speed

  • You start at level 1. Clearing the lines-per-level for your difficulty (12 on Easy, 10 on Normal, 8 on Hard) raises the level by 1.
  • Each level, pieces fall faster, down to a floor of about one row every 0.11 s on Easy, 0.08 s on Normal, and 0.05 s on Hard.
  • There is no level cap; the game ends only when you top out.

Hold rule

  • You may swap the falling piece with the held piece once per drop. After a swap, the hold slot locks until the swapped-in piece lands.
  • If the hold slot is empty, the current piece goes into it and the next piece from the queue falls instead.

Practice rounds

  • Practice is a separate mode, picked in the Mode panel or on the start screen. It can be started any time with the Practice button — starting it replaces the current game.
  • Untimed: no auto-fall timer and no clock. A piece only moves down when you soft-drop or hard-drop it.
  • No score: points, combos, levels, and best scores are not counted or saved in Practice. Lines you clear are tallied for that round only, so you can see how you're doing.
  • Hints on: a suggested placement is shown as an amber outline plus a written hint. It's a suggestion from a simple board check (lines cleared, holes and stack height) — not the only good move.
  • Everything else works the same: same pieces and bag, same hold rule (once per piece), same line clears. If you top out in Practice, the round ends and you can jump straight into another practice round or a scored game — nothing is recorded.

Game over

  • The game ends when a new piece has no room to appear at the top of the well (“topping out”).
  • Your best score is kept on this device so you can chase your own record.
