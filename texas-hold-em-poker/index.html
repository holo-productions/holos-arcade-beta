    How to play — Texas Hold'em

    How to play — under a minute

    1. 1
      The goalWin chips by making the best five-card hand — or by betting so everyone else folds. You start with 1,000 play-money chips. Last player with chips takes the table.
    2. 2
      Your two cardsEveryone gets two private cards. Two players post forced bets each hand — the small blind (10) and big blind (20) — and the dealer button moves one seat every hand.
    3. 3
      Four betting roundsBet before the flop, then as shared cards land: the flop (3 cards), the turn (1) and the river (1). On your turn you can Fold, Check (bet nothing), Call (match the bet), Raise, or go All-in.
    4. 4
      The other seatsBlaze plays aggressive, Nova plays loose, Tex plays tight. They judge their hand strength, watch the pot odds — and yes, they bluff sometimes. Higher difficulty = sharper, trickier AI.
    5. 5
      ShowdownAnyone still in after the river shows their cards and the best hand wins the pot. If someone bets big and everyone folds, they win it without showing. Bust all three rivals to clear the table!
    6. 6
      Practice roundsTap Practice round up top any time. Everyone's cards flip face-up, your coach shows your win chance, pot odds and a nudge on what to try — and busting just refills your stack. Practice wins don't touch your real record, and tapping the button again pops you right back to your real game, chips just how you left them.

    Rules — Texas Hold'em

    The full rules, in plain English

    1. 1
      🎯 The objectiveBe the last player with chips. It's you against three AI rivals — Blaze 🔥 (aggressive), Nova ✨ (loose) and Tex 🤠 (tight) — and everyone starts with 1,000 play-money chips. Win chips hand by hand until the other three are busted. Lose all of yours and the game is over. Play money only — no real gambling.
    2. 2
      🂡 How a hand worksEveryone gets two private cards. The dealer button moves one seat every hand, and the two seats after the dealer post forced bets — the small blind (10) and big blind (20). Heads-up, the dealer posts the small blind and acts first before the flop. There are four betting rounds: pre-flop, then the flop (3 shared cards), the turn (1) and the river (1). Your hand is the best five cards from your two plus the five shared cards.
    3. 3
      🎲 Betting rulesOn your turn: Fold (give up the hand and any chips already bet), Check (pass — only when there's no bet to call), Call (match the current bet), Raise (increase it — a raise must be at least the size of the previous raise, starting at 20), or All-in (every chip you have left). A short all-in that can't cover the current bet counts as a call. Short stacks create side pots: you can only win the pots you put chips into. If everyone folds to a bet, that player wins the pot without showing their cards. Tied hands split the pot — the odd chip goes to the first winner.
    4. 4
      🏆 Winning & losingAt showdown, the best hand wins — Royal Flush down to High Card, and an Ace can play low for a 5-high straight (full list under 🃏 Hand rankings below the table). A player at 0 chips is out. The game ends when one player holds all the chips: bust all three rivals to clear the table and win; bust yourself and it's game over. Your hands won and biggest pot won are saved on this device.
    5. 5
      🤖 Difficulty differencesEasy: the AI plays looser, makes more mistakes, bluffs rarely and bets smaller. Normal: balanced — weighs hand strength and pot odds, bluffs sometimes. Hard: sharper — judges hands more accurately, bluffs more often and bets bigger. The personalities (Blaze aggressive, Nova loose, Tex tight) apply at every difficulty. Changing difficulty applies from the next AI decision.
    6. 6
      🎓 Practice modeTap 🎓 Practice round any time — your real game (chips, button, hand number) is saved exactly as you left it. In practice everyone starts fresh on 1,000 chips, all cards are face-up, and the coach shows your current hand, win chance, pot odds and a suggested move. Busting just refills the stacks, so practice never ends, and nothing in practice touches your records. Tap the button again to return to your real game.
