        How to play Battleship

        Goal: sink all five enemy ships before the computer sinks yours.

        Your fleet

        ShipLength
        Carrier5 squares
        Battleship4 squares
        Cruiser3 squares
        Submarine3 squares
        Destroyer2 squares

        Placing your ships

        • Pick a ship from the tray, then tap a square on Your fleet board to place it.
        • Tap ↻ Rotate ship (or press R) to switch between horizontal and vertical.
        • Ships can't overlap or run off the board — the preview turns red when a spot won't work.
        • Tap a placed ship to pick it back up and move it. In a hurry? Random fleet places everything for you.
        • When all five are placed, tap Start battle.

        Firing

        • Tap any square in Enemy waters to fire. A red ✕ is a hit, a pale dot is a miss.
        • Hit every square of a ship to sink it — the log and the fleet lists announce each sinking.
        • After your shot, the computer fires back at your fleet. First side to sink all five enemy ships wins.

        The computer captains

        • Ensign (easy) — fires at random. A gentle first game.
        • Captain (standard) — hunts at random, but once it scores a hit it targets the squares around it until the ship sinks.
        • Admiral (hard) — hunts on a checkerboard pattern (no ship can hide from it), follows the line of a damaged ship, and remembers the water around ships it has already sunk.

        Practice round

        • Flip the Practice round switch at the top any time to start a relaxed round.
        • In practice the enemy never fires back, there's no timer and no score, and the game doesn't count toward your records.
        • Use Show me a good target for a hint — it points at a square next to a hit, or a smart checkerboard square when you're still hunting.
        • You can also Peek at the enemy fleet to see how ships hide. Turn practice off when you're ready for a real battle.

        Battleship — Full Rules

        The complete rulebook: setup, turns, sinking, scoring, win conditions, and special rules.

        1. Objective & win condition

        • Each side hides a fleet of five ships on a 10 × 10 board (columns A–J, rows 1–10).
        • You win the moment every square of all five enemy ships has been hit — the whole enemy fleet is sunk.
        • You lose the moment the computer has hit every square of all five of your ships.
        • There are no draws: you always fire first each game, so a game cannot end tied.

        3. Placement rules

        • Ships are placed in one straight line, horizontally or vertically — never diagonally and never bent.
        • Ships may touch each other (sides or corners) but may never overlap, and no part of a ship may run off the board.
        • You may move a ship as often as you like before the battle starts: tap a placed ship to pick it back up.
        • Once you tap Start battle, your placement is locked for the rest of that game.
        • The computer's fleet is placed randomly at the start of every game, under the same rules, and stays hidden until you hit it.

        4. Turn & firing rules

        • Turns alternate strictly: one shot for you, then one shot for the computer. A hit does not earn an extra shot.
        • You may fire at any square in Enemy waters that you have not already fired at. Squares already shot are locked — no square can be fired at twice.
        • A shot on a ship square is a hit (red ✕). A shot on open water is a miss (pale dot).
        • A ship is sunk when every one of its squares has been hit. Sinkings are announced immediately in the message bar, the Captain's log, and both fleet lists.
        • Your most recent shot — and the computer's — is outlined in gold on each board.

        5. Scoring & records

        • There is no points score during a game. Your performance is measured by shots and accuracy: accuracy = hits ÷ shots, shown live during the battle.
        • Records saved on this device: games played (finished games only), wins, win rate, fewest shots to win, and best accuracy in a win.
        • Fewest-shots and best-accuracy records only count games you won — a fast loss never sets them.
        • Abandoning a game with New game before it ends does not count as a played game and does not affect your records.
        • Reset records wipes all saved records. It does not change the game in progress.

        6. The computer captains (difficulty rules)

        • Ensign (easy) — every shot is chosen at random from squares it has not tried.
        • Captain (standard) — hunts at random, but after a hit it targets the untried squares next to its hits until that ship is sunk.
        • Admiral (hard) — hunts on a checkerboard pattern, follows the line of a damaged ship to find its ends, and rules out the water around ships it has sunk.
        • The computer never sees your board: it only knows the results of its own shots, exactly as you only know yours. It never fires at the same square twice.
        • Changing difficulty starts a fresh game with new fleets.

        7. Special rules

        • Rematch: after a game ends, Rematch deals two brand-new fleets at the same difficulty — your previous placement is not kept.
        • Defeat reveal: if you lose, the enemy ships you never found are revealed (dimmed) on the Enemy waters board so you can see what you missed.
        • Random fleet is a legal placement: it follows the same no-overlap, on-board rules as manual placement.
        • Practice round: when the Practice switch is on, the enemy holds fire (no return shots), hints and peek are available, and the result is not counted in games, wins, win rate, fewest shots, or best accuracy. Turning practice on or off starts a fresh game.
        • Sound is optional flavour only and never affects play; the toggle is saved on this device.
        • Everything is saved in this browser on this device only — no account, no server, nothing leaves your device.
