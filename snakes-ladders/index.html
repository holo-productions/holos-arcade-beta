Rules — Snakes & Ladders

The complete rules for this version: how a game is won, how scoring and records work, and every special rule.

Win condition

  • The first counter to land exactly on square 100 wins the game, immediately. There is no extra turn, no finish-line photo review — the game ends on that move.
  • Reaching 100 by climbing the ladder from square 80 counts exactly the same as landing on 100 with a dice roll.
  • If nobody has reached 100, nobody has won — the race continues until someone does. There are no draws and no turn limit.

Turns & movement

  • Players take turns rolling one six-sided die and moving their own counter forward that many squares. You (Player 1) always roll first in a new game.
  • Both counters start off the board on “Start” (square 0). The first roll moves the counter onto the board.
  • The board zig-zags: square 1 is bottom-left, numbers run left-to-right along the bottom row, then right-to-left on the row above, alternating all the way up to 100 at the top-left.
  • Two counters may share a square. Landing on your opponent's square has no effect — nobody is knocked off or sent back.

Ladders

  • Ending your move on the foot of a ladder is mandatory climbing: you move immediately to the square at the top of that ladder. You cannot choose to stay.
  • Passing over a ladder's foot during your move does nothing — you must end your move exactly on it.
  • A climb does not count as a new move: if the top of a ladder were a snake's head you would then slide (on this board no ladder top is a snake's head, so climbs never chain).

Snakes

  • Ending your move on a snake's head is a mandatory slide: you move immediately down to the square at its tail. You cannot choose to stay.
  • Passing over a snake's head during your move does nothing — you must end your move exactly on it. That includes passing through square 98 on the way somewhere else.
  • A slide does not count as a new move, and no snake's tail is a ladder's foot on this board, so slides never chain.

Special rules

  • Roll a 6, roll again. Rolling a 6 earns the same player another full turn, taken immediately. Sixes can chain — three 6s in a row means three extra turns. A 6 that ends the game (landing exactly on 100) wins instead; there is no extra turn after a win.
  • Exact finish with bounce-back. You may only land on 100 with the exact number needed. If your roll would take you past 100, you walk up to 100 and then back down the remaining steps. Example: on square 97 you roll a 4 — you walk 98 → 99 → 100 → 99 and end your move on 99. (Passing through 98 on the way does not trigger its snake; only ending a move there would.) If a bounce-back ever ended your move on a snake's head or a ladder's foot, that snake or ladder would apply as normal.
  • The 80 ladder. Landing on square 80 climbs straight to 100 and wins the game on the spot.
  • No choices, no captures. Snakes & Ladders is a game of pure luck in this version: there are no decisions to make, no capturing, and no way to affect the other counter.

Modes

  • You vs Computer — you play against Ruby the Robot. Ruby rolls automatically right after your turn ends. The same rules apply to Ruby, including extra turns on a 6 and the bounce-back.
  • 2 Players — pass-and-play on one device. Player 2 uses the same Roll button on their turn. Switching modes starts a fresh game with both counters back on Start.
  • 🧪 Practice — a solo practice round. Only your counter is on the board, there's no opponent and no turn-taking — every roll is yours. All the normal rules still apply (ladders, snakes, roll-a-6 extra turns, exact finish with bounce-back, and your chosen difficulty), and you still win the round by landing exactly on 100. Practice rounds never count toward your records: no games, wins, best rolls, or ladder counts are saved from them. Tap Play for real any time to switch to a counted game vs Ruby.

Difficulty

  • Easy — the gentle board. Only the five shortest snakes are on the board (16 → 6, 47 → 26, 49 → 11, 56 → 53, 64 → 60); the five long slides (62, 87, 93, 95, 98) are removed. All nine ladders stay.
  • Normal — the classic board listed above: all ten snakes and all nine ladders.
  • Hard — the classic board plus five extra snakes: 25 → 7, 43 → 18, 58 → 35, 74 → 45, 88 → 52. All nine ladders stay.
  • Difficulty changes the board for both players equally, in both modes. Switching difficulty starts a fresh game, and your records cover games played on every difficulty.

Scoring & records

  • There are no points during a game — your square number is your score, and the only result that matters is who reaches 100 first.
  • Your records, saved in this browser on this device only: games finished, wins (your wins vs the computer; Player 1 wins in 2-player games), fewest rolls in a win (your personal best — lower is better), and total ladders climbed across all your games.
  • A game only counts toward your records when someone reaches 100. Starting a new game or switching modes mid-race abandons the old game with nothing recorded.
  • Practice rounds never count toward your records, even when you reach 100 — they're just for warming up and learning the board.
  • Reset stats clears all four records back to zero. It does not affect a game in progress.
