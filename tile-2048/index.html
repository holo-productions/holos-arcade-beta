How to play

2048 is a sliding-tile puzzle. Every move slides all the tiles at once — your job is to combine matching numbers until you build the target tile. The board and target depend on your difficulty (see step 5).

1

Slide the whole board

Press an arrow key (or W A S D), swipe, or tap the direction pad. Every tile slides as far as it can in that direction, and one new tile — usually a 2 — appears in a random empty square.

2

Matching tiles merge

When two tiles with the same number bump into each other, they merge into one tile worth double. Two 4s become an 8, two 8s become a 16, and so on.

44 8 128

Slide left and the two 4s on the top row merge into a single 8.

3

Reach the target before you run out of room

The board fills up fast. If it fills completely and no two neighbouring tiles match, the game is over. A good habit: keep your biggest tile tucked in one corner and build toward it.

4

Made a slip?

The Undo button takes back your last move, including the tile that spawned. Your best score is saved on this device for each difficulty, so you can always chase it next game.

5

Pick your difficulty

Use the Easy / Normal / Hard switch above the board — switching starts a fresh game at that level.

  • Easy: a roomier 5 × 5 board, new tiles are always 2s, and the target is 1024.
  • Normal: the classic 4 × 4 game — new tiles are mostly 2s with the odd 4, target 2048.
  • Hard: the same 4 × 4 board, but new tiles are 4s far more often and the target is 4096.
6

Just want a knockabout? Try a practice round

Flip the Round switch above the board to Practice round. Nothing counts — your best score is safe no matter how it goes — undo is bottomless, and a little coach tip under the board suggests a move to try. Perfect for testing a new corner strategy before it counts. Flip back to Real game when you're ready to make it official.

Rules

Objective

Combine matching numbered tiles to create the target tile for your difficulty: 1024 on Easy, 2048 on Normal, 4096 on Hard. Reaching the target wins the game; you may keep playing afterwards to chase a higher score and even bigger tiles.

2048 is a single-player game — it's just you against the board. There is no opponent and no timer; your only rival is your own best score, which is saved on this device separately for each difficulty.

Difficulty

Pick Easy, Normal, or Hard with the switch above the board. Switching difficulty starts a new game, and your choice is remembered on this device. Everything else — sliding, merging, scoring, and undo — works the same at every level; only the board, the new-tile mix, and the target change:

Practice rounds

Use the Round switch above the board to pick Real game or Practice round. Switching starts a fresh round, and your choice is remembered on this device. Practice rounds play by exactly the same sliding, merging, and spawning rules for your difficulty — the differences are all about pressure, or rather the lack of it:

  • A practice score is never recorded as a best score, win or lose.
  • Undo has no practical limit in a practice round — step back through as many moves as you like.
  • A coach tip under the board suggests a next move after every slide. It's a nudge, not gospel — ignore it whenever you fancy.
  • Filling the board in practice just ends that practice round; undo or start a fresh one and carry on experimenting.

How a move works

  • A move is one slide: up, down, left, or right. All tiles slide as far as they can in that direction until they hit the edge of the grid or another tile.
  • If a sliding tile meets a tile of the same value, the two merge into a single tile of double value in the far tile's square.
  • A tile created by a merge cannot merge again in the same move. Example: 2 – 2 – 4 sliding left becomes 4 – 4, not a single 8.
  • When three or more identical tiles line up, the pair closest to the edge you're sliding toward merges first. Example: 2 – 2 – 2 sliding left becomes 4 – 2.
  • A slide that changes nothing (no tile moves and no merge happens) does not count as a move, and no new tile spawns.

New tiles

  • After every successful move, exactly one new tile appears in a random empty square.
  • What that tile can be depends on difficulty: on Easy it is always a 2; on Normal it is a 2 nine times out of ten and a 4 the other time; on Hard it is a 2 seven times out of ten and a 4 three times out of ten.
  • Every game starts with two tiles already on the board.

Scoring

Your score increases by the value of each tile created by a merge. Merging two 8s into a 16 adds 16 points; merging two 1024s into the 2048 tile adds 2,048 points. Tiles that simply slide, and newly spawned tiles, add nothing.

Your best score is saved on this device separately for each difficulty and carries over between games at that level. The move counter counts successful slides only. Practice-round scores are the one exception — they never become a best score.

Winning and losing

  • Win: create your difficulty's target tile (1024 on Easy, 2048 on Normal, 4096 on Hard). You'll be offered the choice to stop there or keep playing — the game continues with no upper limit on tile values.
  • Game over: the grid is completely full and no two horizontally or vertically neighbouring tiles share a value, so no slide can change the board. Diagonal neighbours do not count.
  • There is no timer and no move limit.

Undo

  • Undo takes back the most recent successful move: the board, score, and move count return to exactly what they were before it, and the tile that spawned after that move is removed.
  • Undo can be used repeatedly to step back through earlier moves, but there is no redo — once you make a new move, the undone path is gone.
  • Undo is available after a game over, so one bad slide doesn't have to end a good run.

Controls

  • Keyboard: arrow keys or W A S D.
  • Touch: swipe on the board, or use the direction pad.
  • Mouse: click and drag on the board, or use the direction pad.
