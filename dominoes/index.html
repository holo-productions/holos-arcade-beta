    Dominoes — Full Rules

    Game: Draw dominoes with a double-six set, two players — you vs Nova (AI). A match is a series of rounds. First to 100 points wins the match.

    1. The set & the deal

    • The set has 28 tiles: every pairing of 0 (blank) to 6, from [0|0] to [6|6]. Each tile's value is its two ends added together — its pips.
    • Each round, the set is shuffled. You draw 7 tiles, Nova draws 7 tiles, and the remaining 14 tiles form the boneyard, face down.
    • You can always see your own tiles and the count of Nova's tiles, never her faces.

    2. Who leads each round

    • Round 1 (and any round after a dead round): whoever holds the highest double must lead with that exact tile. Doubles rank by their face: [6|6] beats [5|5], and so on.
    • If neither hand holds a double, the highest-pip tile leads instead (ties broken by the higher single end).
    • Later rounds: the winner of the previous round leads, and may open with any tile from their hand.

    3. Playing on the chain

    • The played tiles form one chain with two open ends — the exposed number at the far left and the far right.
    • On your turn you must play if you can: choose a tile with an end that matches either open end, and place it on that side. The touching ends must show the same number.
    • If a tile matches both ends, you choose which side to play it on — that choice is real strategy, because it decides which two numbers stay open.
    • Turns alternate. There is no playing out of turn and no skipping a playable tile.

    4. Doubles — the special rule on this table

    • A double (both ends equal, like [4|4]) is placed crosswise on the chain so you can spot it at a glance.
    • On this table a crosswise double still leaves the chain with the usual two open ends — there is no four-way “spinner” branching. The double's number is simply the open end on its side of the chain.
    • Doubles are heavy: [6|6] is worth 12 pips if you're caught holding it, so getting doubles out early is usually wise.

    5. Drawing & passing

    • If no tile in your hand matches either open end, you must draw from the boneyard, one tile at a time, until you draw a tile that plays — then you may play.
    • You may not draw voluntarily while you hold a playable tile, and you may not pass while the boneyard still has tiles.
    • If the boneyard is empty and you still cannot play, you pass and your turn ends.

    6. How a round ends

    • Domino: a player plays their last tile. The round ends immediately.
    • Blocked (locked) chain: both players pass one after the other with the boneyard empty — nobody can play on either end. The round ends immediately.

    Worked example: the chain blocks. You're holding [6|3] and [2|0] = 11 pips. Nova is holding [5|5] and [4|1] = 15 pips. Your total is lower, so you score 15 − 11 = 4 points.

    Points are added to the match score as soon as the round ends. There are no bonus points for the [0|0] tile or for winning a round by a shutout beyond the pips themselves.

    8. Winning the match

    • The first player to reach 100 points or more at the end of a round wins the match. Because only one player can score in any round, there is never a tied match.
    • After a match, Rematch starts a fresh match at 0–0 on the same difficulty.

    9. Difficulty — what changes

    • Difficulty changes only Nova's play, never the deal or the rules. Changing difficulty starts a fresh match.
    • Your difficulty choice is remembered on this device, so the table is set the way you left it on your next visit.
    • Easy: Nova plays a random tile that fits.
    • Normal: Nova sheds her heaviest tiles (and doubles) first, so she's holding fewer pips if the round blocks.
    • Hard: Nova also steers the open ends toward numbers she can follow up on, tries to make both ends equal, and pushes for a block when she's holding fewer pips than you.

    10. Practice round

    • Tap Start practice round (near the top, or the 🎯 Practice round button by the controls) any time to play a single round just for practice.
    • Practice uses the same deal, chain, drawing and blocked-round rules and the same Nova difficulty — only the stakes change.
    • Points from a practice round do not count toward the match to 100, and they do not change Your Bests, win streaks or match counts. The end-of-round message tells you what you would have scored.
    • Starting practice replaces the current match on the table. When you’re done, tap Exit practice to start a fresh real match at 0–0, or play another practice round.

    11. Your bests

    Matches played and won, rounds won, best round score, biggest win margin and win streaks are saved on this device only (browser local storage), shown under “Your Bests”. No account, and nothing leaves your device. Clearing your browser data clears them. Practice rounds never change these numbers.
