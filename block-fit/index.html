Block Fit — Rules

Block Fit is a relaxing block puzzle. Place pieces on the 8 × 8 board, fill complete rows or columns to clear them, and keep the board open as long as you can. There is no timer — the game ends only when none of your pieces fit.

How a turn works

  • You always have 3 pieces in the tray below the board.
  • Drag a piece onto the board, or tap a piece, then tap the board to place it where you tapped (the piece centres on your tap).
  • Pieces cannot overlap other blocks or hang off the edge of the board. They cannot be rotated.
  • When all 3 pieces are used, 3 fresh pieces arrive.
  • The game ends when none of your remaining pieces can fit anywhere on the board. Pieces that no longer fit are dimmed in the tray.

Clearing lines

  • Fill every cell of a row or a column and it clears, freeing that space.
  • One placement can clear several rows and columns at once — that's where the big points are.

Combo: clear lines on back-to-back placements and the line points multiply — ×2 on the second clearing placement in a row, ×3 on the third, and so on. Placing a piece that clears nothing resets the combo to ×1.

Practice round

Tap 🎓 Practice any time to start a practice round. Practice is for learning — it never touches your Best score.

  • No score saved — points are shown just as feedback while you play; nothing is recorded and there is no new-best.
  • 💡 Hint — highlights a piece in the tray and a good spot for it on the board, preferring a spot that clears a line.
  • ↩ Undo move — takes back your last placement, as many times as you like, so you can try a different idea.
  • 🎲 Fresh pieces — swaps your remaining pieces for 3 new ones when you're stuck.
  • When no piece fits, a practice round doesn't have to end — choose Keep practising for fresh pieces on the same board, or start a scored game.
  • Tap 🎓 Practice again (or New game on the end screen) to go back to a normal scored game. Your difficulty stays the same in practice.

Difficulty

  • Easy — smaller, friendlier pieces: singles, dominoes, small corners and 2 × 2 squares.
  • Normal — the full mix, including 3 × 3 squares and 5-long bars now and then.
  • Hard — big, awkward pieces turn up much more often. Plan two moves ahead.

Your difficulty choice and your best score for each difficulty are saved on this device. Changing difficulty starts a fresh game.

Tips

  • Keep the middle of the board open — big pieces need somewhere to land.
  • Don't rush to clear a single line if one more piece could clear two.
  • Watch the dimmed pieces: if a piece stops fitting, work around it before it's too late.
