    How to play — Cribbage

    The classic race around the board — under two minutes

    1. 1
      The goalFirst to 121 points wins. You score by moving pegs around the board — points come from playing cards and from counting your hand at the end. If your opponent finishes under 91, they're skunked(under 61 = double skunk).
    2. 2
      The deal & the cribYou each get 6 cards. Tap 2 of yours to send to the crib — a bonus hand that belongs to the dealer. Feeding your own crib? Throw pairs and 5s. Starving the AI's crib? Throw cards that don't combine. Then a starter card is cut for everyone.
    3. 3
      PeggingTake turns playing cards, keeping the running total at 31 or under. Score as you go: a total of 15 = 2, a pair = 2, a run = 1 per card, exactly 31 = 2, and the last card before a “go” = 1. Can't play without passing 31? Say Go, and the count restarts.
    4. 4
      Counting handsWhen the cards run out, hands are counted with the starter: every 15 = 2, pairs, runs, a flush (4–5), and nobs — a Jack matching the starter's suit = 1. The non-dealer counts first, then the dealer, then the crib. Order matters when both players are near 121!
    5. 5
      Your opponentThe AI discards to the crib, pegs for points, and counts its own hands. Easy plays loosely, Normal plays a solid game, Hard guards its crib and sets traps in pegging. The dealer swaps every hand. Good luck — mind the skunk line!

    Rules — Cribbage

    Full rules: scoring, special rules & win conditions

    🎯 Goal & win conditions

    Cribbage is a race to 121 points. Points are pegged immediately as they are scored — during pegging, when hands are counted, and for special plays like his heels. The first player to reach or pass 121 wins instantly, even if the hand hasn't finished being counted.

    Counting order matters at the finish: the non-dealer (pone) counts first, then the dealer, then the crib. If the pone reaches 121 in their count, they win before the dealer or crib is counted at all.

    Skunk: if the loser finishes on 90 or fewer, they are skunked 🦨. On 60 or fewer it's a double skunk 🦨🦨. The board marks the skunk line at 90. In this game a skunk is bragging rights (and tracked in your records) — the winner is still simply first to 121.

    🂡 Setup & the deal

    • Standard 52-card deck. Aces are always low (A-2-3 is a run; Q-K-A is not).
    • Face cards (J, Q, K) count as 10 for all totals. Other cards count face value; Aces count 1.
    • Each player is dealt 6 cards. The dealer alternates every hand — in this game the first dealer is chosen at random.
    • The non-dealer leads the pegging.

    📥 The crib

    Each player discards 2 cards face-down into the crib, leaving 4 cards in hand. The crib is an extra 4-card hand that belongs to the dealer and is counted last, after both hands. That's why discarding is strategic: feed your own crib with cards that combine (5s, pairs, cards totalling 15), and starve your opponent's crib with cards that don't.

    ⭐ The starter & his heels

    After discards, one card is cut as the starter (also called the turn-up). It stays face-up and counts as a fifth card for every hand — yours, your opponent's, and the crib — when counting.

    His heels: if the starter is a Jack, the dealer immediately pegs 2 points.

    🏃 Pegging (the play)

    Players alternate playing one card at a time, announcing a running total. The total may never exceed 31.

    • Go: if you have no card that keeps the total at 31 or under, you say “Go.” Your opponent keeps playing while they can. When neither player can play, the player who played the last card scores 1 point (2 points instead if the total is exactly 31 — see below).
    • After a go or a 31, the played cards are set aside, the total resets to 0, and the player who did not play last leads the next sequence. Play continues until all 8 hand cards are played.
    • The very last card of the hand also scores 1 point for last card (unless it makes exactly 31, which scores 2 total, not 3).

    Pegging scores (scored by the player who just played):

    • FifteenTotal reaches exactly 15 — 2 pts
    • PairSame rank as the previous card — 2 pts
    • Pair royalThree of a kind in a row — 6 pts
    • Double pair royalFour of a kind in a row — 12 pts
    • RunLast 3+ cards in sequence, in any order (e.g. 4-6-5) — 1 pt per card. A run interrupted by a pair doesn't count; e.g. 2-3-3-4 is a pair only, not a run.
    • Thirty-oneTotal reaches exactly 31 — 2 pts
    • Go / last cardLast card played before a go, or the final card of the hand — 1 pt

    Combinations can stack: a card that makes both 15 and a pair scores for both.

    🧮 Counting the hands (the show)

    When pegging ends, each 4-card hand is scored together with the starter as a 5-card hand, in this order: 1) non-dealer's hand, 2) dealer's hand, 3) the crib. Every distinct combination counts separately.

    • FifteenAny combination of 2–5 cards totalling 15 — 2 pts each. Face cards = 10, so J + 5, 10 + 5, 7 + 8, 2 + 3 + 10 all count.
    • Pair2 pts. Three of a kind = 3 pairs = 6 pts. Four of a kind = 6 pairs = 12 pts.
    • Run3, 4 or 5 cards in sequence — 1 pt per card. Double runs (a run plus a pair, e.g. 7-8-8-9) score both runs plus the pair = 8. Only the longest run length counts.
    • FlushAll 4 hand cards same suit — 4 pts; if the starter matches too — 5 pts. Crib exception: a crib flush scores only when all 5 cards match — 5 pts; a 4-card crib flush scores 0.
    • NobsA Jack in your hand (or the crib) in the same suit as the starter — 1 pt. Also called “his nob.”

    The perfect hand is 29 — three 5s + a Jack of the fourth 5's suit, with that 5 as starter (eight 15s, a pair royal, and nobs). Scores of 19, 25, 26 and 27 are impossible in a hand count — a zero hand is jokingly called a “19 hand.”

    📌 Special rules in this game

    • Play money — no real gambling: this game is points only. There is no real-money wagering, no prizes, and no facilitating side bets — in the game or around it.
    • No muggins: points are counted automatically — you can't steal points your opponent missed.
    • Front peg / back peg: each track shows two pegs — your current score and where you were before your last score, just like a real board.
    • Difficulty (Easy / Normal / Hard) only changes how well the AI discards and pegs. The rules and scoring are identical at every level.
    • Records (matches won, skunks dealt, best hand, fastest win) are your personal bests for this device.
