How to play Minesweeper

Clear the whole field without digging up a mine. The numbers do all the talking — under a minute to learn.

  1. 1
    The goal Reveal every square that doesn't hide a mine. Easy hides 10 mines in a 9 × 9 field; Hard hides 99. You never need to reveal a mine to win.
  2. 2
    Your first tap is always safe Mines are only planted after your first dig, and never under — or next to — that first square. So open with confidence, ideally near the middle for a bigger opening.
  3. 3
    Numbers count touching mines A revealed number says how many mines touch that square, counting all 8 neighbours — sides and corners. A 1 in a corner with only one hidden neighbour left? That neighbour is a mine.
    121✦
  4. 4
    Flag the mines Worked out where a mine is? Flag it so you don't dig it up by accident: switch to Flag mode, long-press the square, or right-click it. The counter at the top tracks mines left = total mines − flags planted.
  5. 5
    Chord to go faster When a revealed number already has that many flags around it, tap the number itself — it digs every other square around it at once. Fast, satisfying… and unforgiving if a flag is in the wrong place.
  6. 6
    Winning & losing Dig a mine and it's over — the field shows every mine, and a ✕ marks any flag that was wrong. Clear every safe square and you win: your time is saved as a best for that difficulty if it's your fastest yet.
  7. 7
    Try a Practice round first Flip Practice ON above the board for a stress-free round: the timer and bests don't count, digging a mine just flags it and lets you keep going, and the Hint button points out a move the numbers prove — a safe square to dig or a mine to flag. Perfect while you're learning the logic. Flip it OFF when you want a real, timed game.

📖 Minesweeper — Full Rules

The complete rulebook: setup, digging, flagging, chording, timing, scoring and win conditions. For the quick 1-minute version, use How to play instead.

1 · Objective

Reveal every square on the field that does not contain a mine, without ever digging up a mine. There is no opponent and no score to beat except your own best time for each difficulty.

2 · The field & difficulties

Changing difficulty, tapping ↻ New game, or tapping the face button abandons the current field and deals a fresh one. A fresh deal counts as a game played for that difficulty.

3 · First-dig safety

Mines are not placed until your first dig. The square you dig first — and all 8 squares touching it — are guaranteed mine-free, so the first dig can never lose and usually opens a small clearing. This guarantee applies to the first dig only: if your first action is planting a flag, the guarantee moves to your first actual dig.

4 · Numbers

  • A revealed number (1–8) is the exact count of mines in the 8 squares surrounding it — up, down, left, right and the four diagonals.
  • A revealed blank square has 0 neighbouring mines. Digging a blank automatically digs every square around it, cascading through connected blanks and stopping at numbers (flood-fill).
  • Numbers are never wrong. If your deduction contradicts a number, a flag somewhere nearby is misplaced.

5 · Digging

  • In ⛏ Dig mode, tap or click a hidden, unflagged square to dig it.
  • Digging a mine loses the game immediately (see §9).
  • Flagged squares cannot be dug — unflag them first. This protects you from mis-taps.
  • Digging an already-revealed blank does nothing; digging a revealed number chords instead (see §7).

6 · Flagging

  • Plant a flag on any hidden square you believe hides a mine: switch to 🚩 Flag mode and tap, long-press (≈½ second) the square, or right-click it. Repeat the action to remove the flag.
  • Flags are unlimited and purely informational — the game never checks them until the end. You may plant more flags than there are mines; the MINES LEFT counter (total mines − flags planted) simply goes negative.
  • You do not need to flag every mine to win — only revealing all safe squares matters (§8).

7 · Chording

  • Tap a revealed number when the number of flags around it exactly equals the number. The game digs every remaining hidden, unflagged square around it at once.
  • If the flags are correct, chording is a safe shortcut. If any flag is misplaced, a chord can dig a real mine and lose the game — the risk is yours.
  • If the flag count doesn't match the number, the tap does nothing.

8 · Winning

  • You win the moment every non-mine square is revealed — for Easy, that's 71 squares; Normal, 216; Hard, 381.
  • On a win, any unflagged mines are flagged automatically, the mine counter reads 000, the face becomes 😎, and the timer stops.
  • Flagging all mines without revealing all safe squares is not a win.

9 · Losing

  • Digging any mine — directly or by chording — ends the game immediately. The face becomes 😵 and the timer stops.
  • The full field is then revealed: every mine is shown 💣 (the one you dug is highlighted red), correctly flagged mines keep their flags, and any flag that was not on a mine is marked ✕.
  • A lost game counts as played, not won. There is no undo — start a new game to try again.

10 · Timer & scoring

  • The timer starts on your first action (dig or flag) and stops the instant you win or lose. It caps at 999 seconds.
  • There are no points. Your score for a game is simply your finishing time — lower is better.
  • Your fastest winning time per classic difficulty is saved as your best, along with games played and won. Custom fields are untimed for records: they save no bests.
  • Bests live in this browser on this device only. Reset bests (under the board) clears them permanently.

11 · Practice rounds

  • Turn Practice ON with the toggle above the board. It starts a fresh field on the same difficulty (or your custom size) — turning it off also starts a fresh, normal field.
  • No records: practice games don't count as played or won, the timer is for curiosity only, and no best time is saved — win or lose.
  • Mines don't end a practice round: if you dig a mine, it's flagged for you, you get a short note, and you keep playing. Chording onto a mine works the same way.
  • Hints: the 💡 Hint button (only in Practice) highlights one move and explains it. It first looks for a move the revealed numbers prove; if none exists yet, it will peek and point to a guaranteed-safe square and say so. Hints are unlimited.
  • You still win a practice round by revealing every safe square — you'll just see a “Practice complete” message instead of a saved best.

12 · Special rules & edge cases

  • Guaranteed opening, not a guaranteed solve: first-dig safety guarantees a safe start, but classic random placement can occasionally leave a genuine 50/50 guess late in a game. That's part of classic Minesweeper.
  • Dense custom fields: if a custom field is too dense for the full 3 × 3 safe zone, the guarantee shrinks to just the first-dug square.
  • Keyboard: press F to switch between Dig and Flag mode, P to toggle Practice, H for a hint in Practice; Esc closes dialogs.
  • Pausing: there is no pause — but the timer only runs during a live game, and leaving the page mid-game simply abandons the field (it still counts as played).
