    How to play — the 1-minute version

    Race all 15 of your checkers around the board into your home board (bottom-right, points 1–6), then bear them off. First to bear off all 15 wins.

    Your turn, step by step

    • 1. Tap Roll dice.
    • 2. Tap one of your light checkers — the points it can legally reach light up green.
    • 3. Tap a highlighted point to move there. One checker per die, or the same checker twice. Doubles = four moves.
    • 4. Land on a lone enemy checker (a blot) to hit it onto the bar. On the bar yourself? You must re-enter before anything else moves.
    • 5. With all 15 checkers home, tap a checker and then your You off tray to bear it off.

    One tip before you start

    • Two or more of your checkers on a point make it safe and block the AI — build those walls in your home board, and don't leave lone checkers exposed when you can avoid it.
    • Pick Difficulty in the side panel: Easy plays casually, Normal plays solid, Hard plays sharp.
    • Want a pressure-free run first? Tap Practice round at the top (or tick it in the side panel): no score is kept, a hint suggests a good move after every roll, and there’s no timer.

    That's enough to play. For the complete rulebook — dice rules, bearing-off details, the doubling cube, and scoring (gammons & backgammons) — tap Rules.

    Backgammon — full rules

    The complete rulebook for this game, exactly as enforced by the move checker. You are the light checkers; the AI plays dark.

    Setup & direction

    • Each side has 15 checkers. Yours start on points 24 (×2), 13 (×5), 8 (×3) and 6 (×5); the AI mirrors this on points 1, 12, 17 and 19.
    • Your checkers travel from point 24 down to point 1, then off the board. The AI travels from point 1 up to point 24. Your home board is points 1–6; the AI's is points 19–24.
    • The pip count is the total number of points all your checkers still have to travel. Both start at 167.

    The dice

    • Each turn rolls two dice. Each die moves one checker that many points; the two dice may move two different checkers or the same checker twice.
    • Doubles are played four times (a roll of 4-4 is four moves of 4).
    • You must use both dice if any legal sequence allows it. If only one die can be played but either die could be played alone, you must play the higher die. If no legal move exists, the turn passes.
    • A point holding two or more enemy checkers is blocked — you cannot land on it, though you may pass over it.
    • You may land on a point that is empty or holds your own checkers (any number), stacking as high as you like.

    Hitting & the bar

    • A single checker alone on a point is a blot. Landing on an enemy blot hits it: that checker goes to the bar in the centre of the board.
    • A player with any checker on the bar must re-enter it before moving any other checker. Re-entry uses one die and lands on the corresponding point of the opponent's home board (for you, a 1 enters on point 24, a 6 on point 19).
    • Re-entry can hit an enemy blot on the entry point. If the entry point is blocked by two or more enemy checkers, that die cannot be used to enter.
    • If neither die can re-enter a bar checker, the whole turn is forfeited. With two checkers on the bar and only one entry possible, the second die is lost unless it can enter the remaining checker.

    Bearing off

    • You may bear off only when all 15 of your checkers are in your home board (points 1–6) and none are on the bar.
    • An exact die bears off a checker from that point (a 3 takes a checker off point 3).
    • A die higher than the point can bear off a checker from your highest occupied point — only if no checker sits on any higher point. (E.g. rolling 6 with checkers only on points 4 and 2 bears one off point 4.)
    • A lower die may instead move a checker within the home board as a normal move.
    • If a checker is hit while you are bearing off, it must re-enter and travel all the way around again; you cannot resume bearing off until all 15 are home once more.

    The doubling cube (optional)

    • The cube starts at 1, centred — either side may offer the first double. Stakes double in sequence: 1 → 2 → 4 → 8 → 16 → 32 → 64 (maximum).
    • A double may be offered only before rolling, at the start of your turn, and only by the player who owns the cube (or either player while it is centred).
    • The player offered the double may accept — the game continues at the new value and the cube passes to them, making them the only player who can offer the next double — or decline, which ends the game immediately: the player who offered wins the game at the cube's previous value (single-game value; no gammon bonus on a declined double).
    • The AI offers doubles when it judges its position strong, and accepts or declines yours by the same kind of judgement.
    • Difficulty (in the side panel): Easy — the AI plays a random legal move and never offers a double. Normal — the AI picks its best-scoring move. Hard — the AI evaluates each move three times for a sharper pick and doubles earlier. Your choice is saved on this device.
    • With the cube switched off, every game is worth exactly 1 point and no doubles can be offered.

    Scoring & win conditions

    • Winning the game: the first player to bear off all 15 checkers wins.
    • Single game (×1): the loser has borne off at least one checker. Worth the cube value.
    • Gammon (×2): the loser has borne off no checkers. Worth twice the cube value.
    • Backgammon (×3): the loser has borne off no checkers and still has a checker on the bar or in the winner's home board. Worth three times the cube value.
    • Points accumulate across games in the match score at the top of the board (You vs AI) until you reset your record. There is no fixed match target — play as many games as you like.
    • Resigning a double conceded by you, or the AI declining yours, scores as a single game at the cube value in effect before the offer.
