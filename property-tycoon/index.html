Property Tycoon — an original property-trading board game.

Short on cash

Sell houses back at half price or mortgage properties (half their price, paid to you now) until you can cover what you owe. Mortgaged properties collect no rent; lift a mortgage later for the mortgage value plus 10%.

How to play

Property Tycoon — Rules

Goal

Be the last player with money. When a player can't pay what they owe — even after selling houses and mortgaging property — they go bankrupt and are out. If that's you (in Standard mode), the game is lost. Bankrupt every rival to win.

On your turn

  • Tap Roll dice and move that many spaces. Passing Start pays you $200.
  • Roll doubles and you roll again — but three doubles in a row sends you straight to Lockup.
  • Land on an unowned property, depot or utility and you may buy it at the listed price. If you pass, it stays on the market.
  • Land on someone else's property and you pay rent. Rent doubles on an unimproved property when its owner holds the whole colour set.
  • Depots charge $25 / $50 / $100 / $200 depending on how many the owner holds. Utilities charge 4× your dice roll (10× if the owner holds both).
  • Taxes, Twist of Fate cards and Neighbourhood Fund cards do what they say.

Building

  • Own every property in a colour set and you can build. Houses go up one at a time, spread evenly across the set; the fifth building on a property becomes a hotel.
  • Build and manage from Your portfolio during your own turn.
  • Houses sell back at half price. Mortgaging a property pays half its price; unmortgaging costs that plus 10%. Mortgaged properties charge no rent, and you can't build on a set while any of its properties is mortgaged.

Lockup

  • Landing on the Lockup corner is just a visit — no penalty.
  • Being sent there is different: on your turn, pay a $50 fine and roll, use a Get Out of Lockup Free card, or roll for doubles to break out. Fail three turns in a row and you pay the fine and move anyway.
  • You still collect rent while in Lockup.

Modes & difficulty

  • Practice: you start with more cash, get buying hints, and a bailout tops you up instead of bankruptcy — you can't lose, so it's the place to learn. Rivals can still go bankrupt, and bankrupting them all still wins.
  • Difficulty changes your rivals: how much cash they start with and how eagerly they buy and build. Your difficulty and mode choices are remembered for next time.
