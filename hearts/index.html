    How to play Hearts — quick start

    You vs Ruby (West), Pearl (North) and Onyx (East). Points are bad: avoid hearts and the Q♠. Lowest score when someone hits 100 wins. For the complete rules, tap Rules.

    In 4 steps

    • 1. Pass: on most hands, tap 3 cards in your hand at the bottom, then tap Pass. The direction is shown in the message bar (left → right → across → hold).
    • 2. Play: whoever holds the 2♣ leads first. On your turn, tap a highlighted card to play it. Dimmed cards can't be played right now.
    • 3. Follow suit: if you have a card in the suit led, you must play one. If you don't, tap any card to discard it.
    • 4. Win low: the highest card of the suit led takes the trick — and any points in it — and leads next. Check totals in the scoreboard below the table.

    On screen

    • The Q♠ has a dashed gold outline so you can always spot her (13 points!).
    • The badge under the trick area shows whether hearts are broken yet.
    • Use the Tutorial button any time for a short step-by-step tour, and New game to restart.
    • Not feeling ready? Hit Practice round up top — it's one no-pressure hand where the points don't count and a coach tip pops up on your turns to nudge you along.

    Hearts — full rules

    Objective & winning

    • Hearts is a 4-player trick-taking game played with a standard 52-card deck. There are no partnerships in this version — every player scores for themselves.
    • Points are penalties. The game ends at the end of the hand in which any player reaches 100 points or more.
    • The player with the lowest total at that point wins. If two or more players tie for the lowest total, the win is shared — there is no tie-break hand in this version.

    Setup & deal

    • Each player is dealt 13 cards. Cards rank from 2 (low) to Ace (high). There is no trump suit.
    • Seating: You (South), Ruby (West), Pearl (North), Onyx (East). Play proceeds clockwise.

    Passing

    Before play, each player passes 3 cards face-down at the same time. The direction rotates every hand in a 4-hand cycle, then repeats:

    Hand in cyclePass to
    1stLeft
    2ndRight
    3rdAcross
    4thNo pass — hold 'em

    You must select your 3 cards before seeing the cards passed to you.

    Playing a trick

    • The player holding the 2♣ must lead it to the first trick.
    • Each player in turn must follow suit — play a card of the suit led — if they hold one. If they hold none, they may discard any other card, subject to the first-trick rule below.
    • The highest card of the suit led wins the trick. Cards in other suits can never win a trick, however high they are.
    • The trick winner collects the 4 cards, takes any points in them, and leads the next trick. A hand is 13 tricks.

    Special rules

    • First trick: no point card — no heart and not the Q♠ — may be played on the first trick, even by a player who cannot follow suit, unless that player's hand contains only point cards.
    • Breaking hearts: hearts may not be led until hearts are broken — a heart is discarded on a trick where another suit was led. Exception: a player whose hand is only hearts may lead one. Once broken, hearts may be led freely for the rest of the hand. Hearts reset to unbroken each new hand.
    • The Queen of Spades: Q♠ is a point card (13 points). She does not break hearts and has no other special power — but A♠ and K♠ are dangerous to hold while she is still out, because a spade lead can force them to win the trick she falls on.
    • No reneging: playing off-suit while holding the suit led is not allowed; the game only offers you legal cards on your turn.

    Scoring

    Card taken in a trickPoints
    Each heart ♥ (13 in the deck)1 point each
    Queen of Spades Q♠13 points
    All other cards0 points
    Total per hand26 points

    Scores are added to each player's running total after every hand and shown on the scoreboard.

    Shooting the moon

    • If one player takes all 26 points in a hand — every heart plus the Q♠ — they have shot the moon.
    • The shooter scores 0 for the hand and each opponent scores +26. (This version uses the add-to-opponents method; the shooter does not have the option to subtract 26 from their own total instead.)
    • Taking 25 of 26 points is not a moon — those 25 points count normally against the player who took them.

    This version

    • Difficulty changes only how the AI plays: Easy plays loosely, Normal plays sound Hearts, Hard also ducks points, protects against the queen and tries to stop a moon shot. The rules above never change.
    • Practice round: tap the Practice round button for a single practice hand. It plays by the exact same rules, but the points don't count toward the race to 100, the hand isn't added to the scoreboard, and your saved bests don't change. Coach tips appear during the hand to suggest plays — handy while you're learning. Leave any time with Exit practice, or jump straight into a real game when the hand ends.
    • Play money only: points in this game are just a score — they have no cash value. There is no real-money wagering, no prizes, and no betting of any kind in this game.
    • Your bests (games, wins, best winning score, fewest points in a hand, moons shot) are saved on this device only.
