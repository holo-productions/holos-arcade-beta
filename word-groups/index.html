Word Groups — Rules

The board holds 16 words. Hidden among them are 4 groups of 4 words, and every word belongs to exactly one group. Each group shares a link: a category, a word pattern, or a phrase the words can complete.

How a turn works

  1. Tap 4 words you think form a group. Tap a selected word again to unpick it.
  2. Press Submit. A correct group locks in at the top of the board, with its link revealed.
  3. A wrong guess costs one mistake. If 3 of your 4 words are right, you'll be told you're one away — but not which word is the odd one out.
  4. Guessing the exact same 4 words twice doesn't cost a mistake — you'll just be reminded you tried them.

Winning and losing

  • Find all 4 groups before your mistakes run out and you win the puzzle.
  • Run out of mistakes and the puzzle ends — the groups you missed are revealed, outlined with a dashed edge.
  • Shuffle rearranges the remaining words. Deselect all clears your picks. Neither costs anything.

Group colours

Groups are coloured by how sneaky they are, easiest first:

  • Yellow — the most straightforward link.
  • Green — clear once you see it.
  • Blue — needs a bit of wordplay.
  • Purple — the trickiest link, often a phrase pattern.

Your difficulty choice is saved on this device, and changing it starts a fresh puzzle. A hint names one word and the group it belongs to — the other three are still yours to find.

Practice rounds

Flip Round to Practice round any time for a no-pressure warm-up on the same difficulty:

  • No mistake limit — wrong guesses are just counted, they never end the round.
  • Endless hints on every difficulty, even Hard.
  • Nothing counts — practice rounds don't touch your played / won totals, fewest mistakes, or fastest win.
  • Found all 4 groups? You still get the win moment — it just stays between you and the board.
  • Switching back to Real deal starts a fresh scored puzzle.

Your bests

Games played, games won, your fewest mistakes, and your fastest win are kept for each difficulty on this device, shown under the board. Practice rounds are never counted.
