Holo’s Arcade

How to play

Tempo Trainer tests your inner metronome. You hear a tempo, it stops, and you have to keep it alive with your taps.

  1. Pick a difficulty, then press Start game.
  2. Listen: the metronome clicks at the target tempo for a few beats. Count along in your head — “1, 2, 3, 4”.
  3. Tap: the clicks stop and the big TAP pad lights up. Keep tapping at exactly the same speed until you’ve tapped the required number of beats.
  4. See your drift score: how close your average tempo was, and how steady your taps were.
  5. Press Next round — each round the target tempo rises. There are 5 rounds per game.

Just warming up?

Hit Practice round instead of Start game. You get one round at a surprise tempo in your difficulty range, with the full drift and stability feedback — but no points are added to your score and your best score is untouched. Practice as many times as you like, then jump into a real game when you’re ready.

Pick a difficulty

  • Easy: 60–90 BPM, hear 6 beats, tap 8 beats, generous scoring.
  • Normal: 80–120 BPM, hear 5 beats, tap 10 beats.
  • Hard: 100–150 BPM, hear 4 beats, tap 12 beats, strict scoring.
  • Difficulty can be changed between games, never mid-game. Your choice is saved on this device.

Quick tips

  • Keep counting in your head after the clicks stop — don’t just “feel” it.
  • Subdivide: think “1-and-2-and” to stay locked in.
  • Tap with the same finger or thumb every beat for steadier timing.

Rules

Objective

Match a target tempo from memory. Each round you hear a metronome at the target BPM (beats per minute), then it stops and you tap the same tempo yourself. Score as many points as you can across 5 rounds.

How a round works

  • Listen phase: the metronome plays the target tempo for a set number of beats (Easy 6, Normal 5, Hard 4). The first beat of every 4 is accented. The TAP pad is locked.
  • Tap phase: the metronome stops. Tap the TAP pad (or press Spacebar) for the required number of beats (Easy 8, Normal 10, Hard 12). Your first tap starts the clock — only the gaps between taps are measured.
  • Result: your round score (0–100), your average tempo, your drift, and your stability are shown. Then the next round begins at a higher target tempo.
  • Target tempos rise every round within the difficulty’s BPM range, so later rounds are faster and harder to hold.

Scoring

  • Accuracy (60 points): based on your average error — the difference between your average tap interval and the target interval. Dead-on average = full points; the further your average tempo drifts from the target, the fewer points.
  • Stability (40 points): based on how consistent your tap-to-tap gaps are (standard deviation). Even, metronome-like taps score full points; wobbly taps lose points even if the average is right.
  • Round score = accuracy + stability, rounded, from 0 to 100. Game score = the sum of all 5 rounds (max 500).
  • How forgiving the scoring is depends on difficulty: Easy tolerates larger errors, Hard demands near-perfect timing.
  • Your best game score is saved on this device and shown on the scoreboard.

Difficulty

  • Easy: targets 60–90 BPM, 6 listen beats, 8 taps, wide tolerance.
  • Normal (default): targets 80–120 BPM, 5 listen beats, 10 taps, medium tolerance.
  • Hard: targets 100–150 BPM, 4 listen beats, 12 taps, tight tolerance.
  • Difficulty is chosen before a game starts and cannot be changed mid-game. Your last choice is remembered between visits.

Practice rounds

  • A practice round is a single warm-up round: same listen-then-tap flow, at a random tempo inside your difficulty’s BPM range.
  • You still get a round score, drift, and stability feedback — but it is not added to your game score and never changes your saved best score.
  • Practice as often as you like, before or between games. Starting a real game works exactly the same as always.

Winning & game end

  • A game is 5 rounds. After round 5 you get your final total out of 500.
  • There is no way to “lose” a round — every round scores something. You win by beating your own best score.
  • All metronome and tap sounds are synthesized in your browser with WebAudio. No songs, recordings, or samples are used.
