Rules — Euchre

The game

Euchre is a trick-taking card game for 4 players in two fixed partnerships. Partners sit opposite each other. In this game you partner with Pip against Rex & June. Each hand, one team names a trump suit and tries to win at least 3 of the 5 tricks.

The deck & the deal

  • Euchre uses a 24-card deck: 9, 10, J, Q, K and A in each of the four suits (♠ ♥ ♦ ♣). All other cards are removed.
  • The dealer deals 5 cards to each player. The remaining 4 cards form the kitty, and the top one is turned face up — the up-card.
  • The deal rotates one seat to the left after every hand.

Choosing trump — round 1

  • Starting with the player left of the dealer, each player in turn may order up the up-card (making its suit trump) or pass.
  • If anyone orders it up, the dealer picks up the up-card and discards one card face down, back to 5 cards.
  • The player who orders it up is the maker for that hand.

Choosing trump — round 2 & “stick the dealer”

  • If all four players pass in round 1, the up-card is turned down and a second round begins.
  • In round 2, each player in turn may name any suit except the turned-down suit as trump, or pass.
  • If the turn comes back around to the dealer, the dealer must name a trump suit — passing is not allowed. This is called stick the dealer, so every hand has a maker.

The bowers — the two best cards

  • The right bower is the Jack of the trump suit — the highest card in the game.
  • The left bower is the Jack of the other suit of the same colour (e.g. if ♥ is trump, the J♦ is the left bower). The left bower counts as a trump card in every way — it is played and followed as trump, not as its printed suit.
  • Full trump ranking, high to low: Right bower › Left bower › A › K › Q › 10 › 9.
  • In a plain (non-trump) suit the ranking is A › K › Q › J › 10 › 9 — except that suit's Jack may be missing if it has become the left bower.

Playing the tricks

  • The player to the left of the dealer leads the first trick (unless that player is sitting out — see Going alone).
  • Each player in turn plays one card. You must follow the suit led if you can — and remember, the left bower follows trump. If you can't follow suit, you may play any card, including trump.
  • The trick is won by the highest trump played; if no trump was played, by the highest card of the suit led. Cards of other suits can never win a trick.
  • The winner of each trick leads the next one. A hand is 5 tricks.

Going alone

  • When making trump — in either round — a player may declare they are going alone. Their partner sits the hand out and plays no cards; the maker plays 1 against 2 and tricks have 3 cards.
  • A loner who takes all 5 tricks scores 4 points; 3–4 tricks scores the usual 1 point; 2 or fewer means the loner is euchred (see scoring).

Winning the match

Points are scored after every hand and added to the running total. The first team to reach 10 points wins the match. Only one team scores on any given hand, so there's never a tie at the line.

Special rules & notes for this version

  • Stick the dealer is always on — a hand is never thrown in; somebody always makes trump.
  • No reneging: if you hold a card of the suit led, the game only lets you play a card of that suit (the left bower counts as trump).
  • When a loner's partner sits out, the lead passes to the next active player if the sitting-out player would have led.
  • Difficulty (top of the page) changes how sharply all three AI players — Pip included — bid and play.
  • Practice mode (top of the page): play hands against the same AI with no match score — hand points are shown but never counted, nothing is added to your match record, and a new practice hand is dealt automatically after each one. Switch back to Match any time to start a fresh match to 10.
  • Play money only: points are just for keeping score — there is no real-money wagering, no prizes, and no side bets anywhere in this game.
  • Your match record (wins, matches played, best winning margin) is kept in this browser's local storage only.
