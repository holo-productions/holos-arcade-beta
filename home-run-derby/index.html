Rules

How Home Run Derby works, start to finish.

The goal

You get 10 pitches per round. Time your swing to hit each ball as far as you can. Your score is your total distance plus bragging rights for every home run — any hit that travels 380 ft or more.

How to play

  • Press Throw pitch. The ball travels toward the plate and the needle sweeps across the timing bar.
  • Press Swing! (or tap the field, or press Space) the moment the needle reaches the gold zone.
  • The closer to the centre of the gold zone, the harder and farther you hit it. Dead centre can clear 480 ft.
  • Swing too early or too late and you'll foul it off, hit a weak grounder, or miss completely. Don't swing at all and it's a called strike — that pitch is gone.

Pitch types

  • Fastball — straight and quick; the needle moves at a steady pace.
  • Curveball — the needle speeds up and slows down as the ball breaks. Watch the ball, not just the bar.
  • Changeup — looks like a fastball, arrives late. It starts fast, then floats. Don't jump early.

Practice round

Tap Practice round above the scoreboard any time to switch to practice. Practice is endless and untimed in the sense that there's no round limit and no pressure: pitches come in slower, a coach hint after every swing tells you whether you were early, late, or dead on, and no score is kept — practice hits never touch your saved bests. Tap Exit practice to go back to a scored 10-pitch round. Your difficulty still applies in practice, so you can rehearse on Easy and then try the same timing on Hard.

Scoring details

  • Distance is measured in feet, from home plate to where the ball lands.
  • A swing with perfect timing maxes out around 480–505 ft. Solid contact lands roughly 340–430 ft.
  • Back-to-back home runs build your HR streak — it resets on any non-homer.
  • After 10 pitches the round ends and your totals are checked against your saved bests.
