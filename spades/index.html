How to play Spades

The game in 20 seconds: You and your partner (North) play against West & East. Each hand you bid how many of the 13 tricks you'll win, then play cards to make it. First team to 500 points wins.

1 · Bid

  • Look at your 13 cards and tap a bid: 1–13 tricks, or Nil (0) if you think you can take none at all.
  • Your bid adds to your partner's — together you have to make the total.

2 · Play

  • Tap a glowing card to play it. You must follow suit if you can — the game only lights up legal cards.
  • ♠ Spades are trump and beat every other suit. The trick winner leads the next trick.

3 · Score

  • Make your team bid to score; miss it and you lose points. Extra tricks ("bags") add up — every 10 bags cost −100, so don't get greedy.

🎓 Practice round

  • Tap 🎓 Practice round any time for a single no-pressure hand — great for a first game or testing a brave Nil bid. You'll still see the full scoring breakdown at the end, but nothing is recorded: no points race, no bests, no bags carried over.

Quick tips

  • Count aces and kings as likely tricks, plus long spades.
  • If your partner bid Nil, lead high cards to cover them so they can duck underneath.

Spades — full rules

Four players in two fixed partnerships: You (South) & Partner (North) against West & East. Partners sit across from each other and combine their bids and tricks. A standard 52-card deck is used; cards rank 2 (low) to Ace (high), and ♠ spades are always trump.

Setup & deal

  • Each player is dealt 13 cards. The dealer rotates one seat to the left after every hand.
  • Bidding starts with the player to the dealer's left and goes around the table once — no passing, no changing your bid.
  • The player to the dealer's left also leads the first trick of the hand.

Bidding

  • Each player bids the number of tricks they personally expect to win: 1–13, or Nil.
  • The two partners' bids are added together into one team contract. Example: you bid 4, your partner bids 3 → your team must win at least 7 of the 13 tricks.
  • Nil (bid 0): a solo side-bet that you personally will win zero tricks in the whole hand. It does not change your partner's bid, and your partner plays normally.
  • There is no Blind Nil in this version — you always bid after seeing your cards.

Playing tricks

  • A hand is 13 tricks. The leader plays any legal card; play continues clockwise.
  • Every player must follow suit — play a card of the suit led — if they hold one. Only when you are out of that suit may you play something else.
  • The highest card of the suit led wins the trick, unless a spade is played: any spade beats any card of another suit, and the highest spade wins if more than one is played.
  • The winner of each trick leads the next one.

Special rule — breaking spades

  • Spades cannot be led until they are broken: someone who is out of the suit led has discarded a spade on an earlier trick.
  • The one exception: if spades are the only cards left in your hand, you may lead one.
  • Playing a spade when you are void in the suit led (trumping in) is always allowed — that's what breaks them.

Scoring

  • Made the contract: score 10 points per bid trick, plus 1 point per overtrick (each trick won beyond the bid). Example: bid 7, win 9 → 70 + 2 = 72.
  • Set (missed the contract): score −10 points per bid trick, and no points for tricks won. Example: bid 7, win 6 → −70.
  • Nil made (zero tricks taken): +100 to your team. Nil failed (took 1 or more tricks): −100. Nil is scored separately, on top of your partner's contract result — a failed Nil's tricks still count toward the team contract.
  • If both partners bid Nil, each Nil is scored on its own (+100 or −100 each) and there is no team contract.

Bags 🛍️

  • Every overtrick is a bag. Bags carry over from hand to hand — they are not reset when a hand ends.
  • Every time a team's running bag total reaches 10, that team loses 100 points and 10 bags are removed from its count. This can happen more than once in a game.
  • Tricks taken by a player whose Nil failed are not bags for the Nil itself, but they do count toward the team contract and any overtricks there still become bags.

Winning the game

  • The first team to reach 500 points or more at the end of a hand wins the game. Scores are only finalised at the end of a hand — passing 500 mid-hand doesn't end anything.
  • If both teams pass 500 on the same hand, the higher score wins.
  • If both teams pass 500 with exactly equal scores, another hand is played to break the tie.

Difficulty (AI only — the rules above never change)

  • Easy: AI bids loosely and sometimes wastes high cards.
  • Normal: solid AI — follows suit smartly and protects Nil bids.
  • Hard: AI counts the trump, sets up bag traps, and bids Nil only with a truly weak hand.
