    How to play — Go Fish

    How to play — under a minute

    1. 1
      🎯 The goalCollect books — all four cards of one rank (four 7s, four Kings…). Most books when the deck runs out wins. There are 13 books in total.
    2. 2
      🃏 Your cardsWith 3 rivals you get 5 cards each; with 2 rivals, 7 each. The rest of the deck is the pond in the middle. Pairs and triples in your hand are gold — they're what you ask about.
    3. 3
      🎣 On your turn, askTap one of your cards to pick its rank, then tap a rival. You can only ask for a rank you're holding. If they have any, they must hand over all of them — and you ask again!
    4. 4
      🌊 “Go fish!”No luck? You draw one card from the pond. If it's the rank you just asked for, you get to ask again. Otherwise your turn ends and the next player fishes.
    5. 5
      🧠 Watch and rememberEvery ask gives away information: whoever asks for 9s is holding 9s. The AI listens too — on Hard it remembers everything it hears. Beat them to the books!

    🎓 Want a no-pressure first round? Switch Mode (top of the page) to Practice. Practice rounds don't count toward your records — no wins, losses or streak changes — and you get a coaching tip on each of your turns.

    Rules — Go Fish

    The full rulebook · scoring, special rules & win conditions

    🎯 Objective

    Collect the most books. A book is all four cards of the same rank — for example, all four 7s or all four Kings. There are 13 books in a standard 52-card deck (Aces through Kings). The player with the most books when the game ends wins.

    🃏 Setup

    • Standard 52-card deck, shuffled. Suits don't matter for scoring — only rank matters.
    • 3 rivals (4 players): 5 cards each, 32 cards left in the pond.
    • 2 rivals (3 players): 7 cards each, 31 cards left in the pond.
    • The remaining cards form the face-down draw pile — the pond — in the centre.
    • If you're dealt all four of a rank, that book is laid down straight away. You always go first.

    🎣 How a turn works

    • On your turn, pick one rival and ask for one rank. You may only ask for a rank you are already holding at least one card of.
    • If they have it: they must hand over all cards of that rank — no holding back, no partial gives. You then take another turn and may ask again (any rival, any rank you hold).
    • If they don't have it: they say “Go Fish!” and you draw the top card from the pond. Your turn then passes to the next player — unless you hit the lucky draw below.
    • There is no limit to how many times you can ask in a row while you keep succeeding.

    📚 Books

    As soon as you hold all four cards of a rank, they are removed from your hand and scored as one book. Books are public — everyone can see how many books each player has, but not which cards remain in anyone's hand.

    ⭐ Special rules in this version

    • Lucky catch: if the card you draw from the pond after a “Go Fish!” is exactly the rank you just asked for, you keep your turn and ask again.
    • Empty hand: if your hand is empty at the start of your turn and the pond still has cards, you draw one card and play on. If you book out your hand mid-turn, you draw one card from the pond to keep going (if any remain).
    • Empty pond: once the pond is empty there are no more draws — a failed ask simply ends your turn.
    • Out of the game: a player with no cards and an empty pond sits out; play continues with the remaining players.
    • Honest asks only: you can never ask for a rank you don't hold. Every ask is public, so asking also reveals that you're holding that rank — memory is part of the game.

    🏆 Scoring & winning

    • Scoring is books only: 1 book = 1 point. Leftover cards in hand at the end are worth nothing, and no rank is worth more than any other.
    • Game end: the game ends when all 13 books have been made, or when no player can take a turn (all hands empty and the pond empty).
    • Winner: the player with the most books wins. If two or more players tie on books, the game is a tie — no tiebreaker in this version.
    • Your records: your best haul (most books in one game), games won, games played and current win streak are saved on this device. A tie does not count as a win and ends a win streak. Practice rounds (see below) never touch these records.

    🎓 Practice rounds

    Use the Mode switch at the top of the page to play a practice round instead of a scored game. The cards, rivals and rules are exactly the same — only the record-keeping changes.

    • Records paused: a practice round never changes your games played, games won, win streak or best haul — win or lose, it doesn't count.
    • Coaching tips: on your turns, a tip under your hand points out the strongest ask the table talk has revealed (for example, who asked for a rank you're holding), or suggests your biggest group when nothing has been given away yet.
    • Switching modes starts a fresh round right away, just like changing Rivals or Difficulty. Your Mode choice is remembered on this device.
    • Ready for it to count? Tap Switch to Play in the gold practice banner and the next round is scored as normal.

    🤖 Rivals & difficulty

    • Easy: rivals mostly ask about their biggest group and only sometimes use what they've heard.
    • Normal: rivals usually remember recent asks, but their memory fades after a few turns.
    • Hard: rivals remember everything they hear for the whole game and target known cards.
    • Changing Rivals or Difficulty starts a fresh game immediately.
    • Your Rivals and Difficulty choices are remembered on this device, so the table is set up the same way next time you play.
