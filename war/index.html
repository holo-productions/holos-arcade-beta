How to play ▾
  1. Battle: Tap Battle — you and Nova each flip your top card. The higher card wins, and the winner takes both cards. Aces are high.
  2. War: A tie means WAR. You each bury cards face-down (2 on Easy, 3 on Normal, 4 on Hard), then flip one more. Higher flip takes the whole pot. Tie again? War again — the pot keeps growing.
  3. Win the game: Take all 52 cards. If nobody has them all after 300 rounds, whoever holds the most cards wins.
  4. Auto-play: Feeling hands-free? Turn on Auto-play and watch the battles fly. Tap it again to take back control.
  5. Practice rounds: Flip Mode to Practice rounds up top for a no-pressure warm-up. Same cards, same wars, same difficulty — but practice games don't count toward your bests, streaks, or win rate. Perfect for learning the ropes or just flipping for fun. Switch back to Real game when you're ready to make it count.

Difficulty (top of the page) changes two things — your starting hand and how big each war gets: Easy deals you 30 cards to Nova's 22 and wars bury 2 cards each, Normal is the classic even 26–26 with 3 buried, and Hard flips the deal — you start with 22 to Nova's 30 — and wars bury 4 each, so pots swing much bigger. Your pick is remembered on this device, and changing it starts a fresh game.

Your wins, streaks, and fastest victory are saved as bests on this device, below the table.

Rules ▾

Objective

Capture the entire 52-card deck. There are no points in War — cards are the score. The card counts at the top of the table are the scoreboard, and the meters show each side's share of the deck.

The deck & card rankings

A standard 52-card deck: four suits (♠ ♥ ♦ ♣), ranks 2 through Ace. Cards rank 2 (low) → 3 → 4 → 5 → 6 → 7 → 8 → 9 → 10 → Jack → Queen → King → Ace (high). Suits never matter — only rank is compared, so a King of clubs beats a Queen of hearts, and two 7s always tie no matter their suits.

Setup

The deck is shuffled and dealt face-down into two piles. Your starting pile and the size of each war depend on difficulty — your pick is remembered on this device, and changing it starts a fresh game:

Battles

  • Each Battle tap (or Space / Enter) flips the top card of each pile at the same time. One flip = one round.
  • The higher rank wins the battle. The winner takes all cards in the pot — both flipped cards, plus anything built up by wars.
  • Won cards are shuffled before going to the bottom of the winner's pile, so a card you just won can come back at any time.

War (ties)

  • When both flipped cards have the same rank, it's war. Both cards stay in the pot.
  • Each side buries cards face-down into the pot — 2 each on Easy, 3 each on Normal, 4 each on Hard — then flips one more card. The higher flip takes the entire pot.
  • If the deciding cards tie again, war repeats — another set of face-down cards, another flip, and the pot keeps growing until someone wins it.
  • Short piles: a side always keeps at least 1 card back to flip, so with few cards left you may bury fewer than the full amount for your difficulty.
  • Running out mid-war: if you have no card left to flip, Nova takes the pot and the game ends. If Nova runs out, you take the pot and the game ends. If both sides run out at once, the game is decided on total cards held.

Winning the game

  • Take all 52 cards and you win the war outright.
  • Round limit: if nobody holds all 52 after 300 rounds, the game ends and whoever holds the most cards wins.
  • If both sides hold exactly the same number when the limit hits, the game is a dead-even tie.
  • Running out of cards at any point loses the game.

Practice rounds

Mode is picked at the top of the page and remembered on this device. Real game is the scored game described above. 🎯 Practice rounds plays exactly the same way — same deal for your difficulty, same war burns, same 300-round limit — but the result is not recorded: no win, loss, streak, or fastest-win is added to your bests. Changing mode starts a fresh game, just like changing difficulty. The gold strip under the controls and the gold ring around the table show when you're in practice.

Auto-play

Auto-play battles for you on a timer, wars included. Tapping Battle or the Auto-play button again hands control straight back to you. Auto-play stops on its own when the game ends.

Your bests

Finished games are recorded on this device only: games played, wins, losses, win rate, current and best win streaks, your fastest win (fewest rounds), and the most wars in a single game. A dead-even tie doesn't count as a win. Starting a new game mid-game doesn't record anything — only finished games count.
