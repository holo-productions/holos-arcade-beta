How to play Blackjack

Goal in 10 seconds: get closer to 21 than the dealer without going over. Over 21 is a bust — you lose.

Play money — no real gambling. Every bet on this page uses play-money chips with no cash value.

Blackjack — Full Rules

Complete rules for this game: scoring, payouts, dealer play, special actions and win conditions. The short How to play covers the basics — this page is the reference.

1. Objective & win conditions

  • Beat the dealer in one of three ways: (a) finish with a total closer to 21 than the dealer without busting, (b) the dealer busts (goes over 21) while you don't, or (c) be dealt Blackjack when the dealer isn't.
  • You lose if you bust, or if the dealer finishes closer to 21 than you.
  • A push (tie) returns your bet — nobody wins or loses on that hand.
  • Each split hand is judged separately against the dealer.

2. Card values & scoring

  • Number cards (2–10) = face value. Jack, Queen, King = 10.
  • Ace = 11 or 1, whichever gives the better hand without busting.
  • A hand where an Ace counts as 11 is soft (e.g. A+6 = soft 17). A hand with no Ace, or an Ace forced to 1, is hard.
  • Blackjack (natural) is exactly an Ace + a 10-value card as your first two cards on an unsplit hand. A 21 made with 3+ cards, or on a split hand, is just 21 — not Blackjack.

3. Dealing & turn order

  • Place a bet at or above the table minimum, then Deal. You receive two cards face up; the dealer receives one face up and one face down (the hole card).
  • If the dealer shows an Ace, insurance is offered before anyone plays (see §6). If the dealer shows an Ace or a 10-value card, the dealer peeks for Blackjack.
  • If the dealer has Blackjack, the hand ends immediately. If you also have Blackjack, it's a push (insurance, if taken, still pays).
  • If you have Blackjack and the dealer doesn't, you're paid immediately and don't play the hand out.
  • Otherwise you play each of your hands in turn, left to right if you split. When all your live hands are done, the dealer reveals the hole card and plays.

4. Player actions

  • Hit: take one more card. You may hit repeatedly until you stand or bust.
  • Stand: end that hand with its current total.
  • Double down: available on your first two cards of a hand only. You double that hand's bet (you must have enough bank), take exactly one more card, then automatically stand. If that card busts you, you lose the doubled bet.
  • Split: if your first two cards have the same value (any pair, or any two 10-value cards), you may pay a second bet equal to the first and split them into two hands. Each hand gets one additional card. You may re-split up to a maximum of 4 hands total.
  • Split Aces: each Ace hand receives exactly one card and then must stand — no hits, no re-split of Aces beyond the normal hand limit, and a resulting 21 is not Blackjack.
  • Double after split (DAS): doubling on the first two cards of a split hand — allowed on Casual and Standard tables, not on High Roller (see §8).
  • Surrender: available only as your very first decision on an unsplit two-card hand, where the table allows it. You forfeit the hand and get half your bet back (rounded down to the dollar). The hand does not play out.

5. Dealer rules

  • The dealer reveals the hole card only after all player hands are finished, surrendered, or bust.
  • If every player hand has bust or surrendered, the dealer does not draw.
  • Otherwise the dealer must hit until reaching at least 17.
  • Soft 17 (e.g. A+6): on the Casual table the dealer stands on all 17s. On Standard and High Roller the dealer hits soft 17 and stands on hard 17 and soft 18+.
  • If the dealer exceeds 21, the dealer busts and every live (non-bust, non-surrendered) hand wins.

6. Insurance

  • Offered only when the dealer's face-up card is an Ace and you don't have Blackjack.
  • Cost: exactly half your original bet for that hand (requires sufficient bank). It is a separate side bet that the dealer has Blackjack (a 10-value hole card).
  • If the dealer has Blackjack, insurance pays 2 to 1 — you receive your insurance stake back plus twice it as winnings. If the dealer doesn't have Blackjack, the insurance bet is lost and the main hand continues.
  • Insurance never changes the result of your main hand.

9. Shoe, reshuffle & play money

  • Cards are dealt from a shuffled multi-deck shoe. When less than 25% of the shoe remains at the start of a hand, a fresh full shoe is shuffled in.
  • Play money — no real gambling. This game is play-money only: no real-money wagering, no real-money prizes, and the game does not facilitate side bets between players. All chips, bank balances, bets, payouts and rebuys are play money with no cash value and cannot be bought, sold, transferred, or redeemed.
  • If your play bank falls below the table minimum you can rebuy +$1,000 free play money.
  • Records are saved on this device only (hands played, wins, best streak, best bank).
  • Practice round: turn it on above the table to play with practice chips instead. Practice hands never touch your play bank and never count toward your records, strategy hints stay on even if you've toggled them off, and there is no timer. You can switch practice on or off between hands; your bank is restored exactly as you left it when you switch back.

10. Strategy hints

Strategy hints (toggle above the table) suggest the basic-strategy play for the current hand and table rules — the mathematically best decision over millions of simulated hands. They are advice only; you can always play differently. Anchors: always split Aces and 8s, never split 10s or 5s, stand on hard 17+, double 11 against anything except an Ace, and basic strategy says to decline insurance.
