    M Mystery Manor — Rules

    The case

    Something has gone missing at Aldermere Manor, and the culprit left no witnesses — only a sealed envelope. The envelope hides exactly three cards: one suspect, one room, and one item. Every other card is dealt out to you and two rival detectives. Whatever nobody holds must be in the envelope. Deduce all three and accuse before a rival does.

    On your turn

    1. Walk. Tap any room on the manor plan to move there. You may move once per turn, and you may also stay where you are.
    2. Suggest. Name a suspect and an item. The room in your suggestion is always the room you are standing in. Rivals are asked in turn: the first one holding any of the three named cards must show you one of them, in secret. If nobody can show a card, that tells you something too.
    3. End your turn. Each rival then takes a turn of their own. If a rival's suggestion names a card in your hand, you must choose one matching card to show them.

    Accusing

    Tap Accuse! on your turn and name the suspect, room and item you believe are in the envelope. In a Case game there are no second chances: a correct accusation wins the case, a wrong one loses it on the spot. Rivals play by the same rule — if a rival accuses correctly first, you lose.

    The notebook

    Your notebook lists every card in the game. Cards in your hand and cards a rival has shown you are marked automatically. When a rival shows a card to another rival you will not see which card it was — but the log tells you a card changed hands, and the three cards in that suggestion are worth a second look. Use the Mark button to record your own hunches.

    Difficulty

    • Easy — Rivals are forgetful: they never learn from an unanswered suggestion, and you start the case with one bonus clue (a rival's card revealed to you). Hints are free of extra fuss.
    • Normal — Rivals think things through. If a rival's suggestion goes completely unanswered, they will piece it together and accuse on their next turn.
    • Hard — Rivals piece an unanswered suggestion together immediately, plan their questions around what they still don't know, and may gamble on an early accusation when only a few possibilities remain. A wrong gamble knocks that rival out of the race (they still have to show cards).

    Practice mode

    Practice is the same manor with the pressure off. Rivals still ask questions and show cards, but they never accuse. A wrong accusation in Practice simply tells you how many of the three you got right, costs you 100 points, and the case goes on. Hints are unlimited in spirit — use them and learn the ropes.

    Scoring

    A case starts at 1,000 points. Each full round costs 20, each hint costs 30, and each wrong Practice accusation costs 100. Solving on Hard multiplies your score by 2, on Normal by 1.5. Your best score for each mode and difficulty is saved on this device.

    The people, rooms and items

    Suspects: Professor Wren Holloway (botanist), Chef Marigold Vance, Captain Felix Okafor (retired pilot), Dr. Ines Calder (astronomer), Jasper Thorn (clockmaker), Lady Opal Reyes (opera singer).

    Rooms: the Conservatory, the Library, the Observatory, the Gear Room, the Grand Kitchen and the Music Hall.

    Items: the Crystal Vial, the Iron Key Wrench, the Marble Bust, the Velvet Rope, the Antique Compass and the Gilded Metronome.
