How to play Checkers

Checkers — Full Rules

Objective

Capture all 12 of your opponent's pieces, or block them so they have no legal move on their turn. The first player to do either wins the game. There are no draws in this version — a blocked player loses.

Setup

  • The board is 8 × 8. Pieces sit only on the dark squares.
  • Each side starts with 12 pieces on the three rows nearest them. You play red from the bottom; the computer plays black from the top.
  • Red (you) always moves first.

Moving

  • A regular piece moves one square diagonally forward (toward the opponent) into an empty dark square.
  • Pieces may never move sideways, backwards (unless crowned), or onto a light square.
  • Only one piece may be moved per turn, except during a multi-jump (below).

Capturing

  • To capture, jump diagonally over an adjacent enemy piece and land on the empty dark square immediately beyond it. The jumped piece is removed from the board.
  • Captures are mandatory. If any of your pieces can jump, you must make a capture that turn — you may choose which available capture to take, but you cannot make a quiet move instead.
  • Multi-jumps are mandatory. If the piece you just jumped with can jump again from its landing square, you must continue jumping with that same piece until it can jump no further. Each jumped piece is removed as you pass over it.
  • Regular pieces capture forward only; kings capture in all four diagonal directions.

Crowning (Kings)

  • A piece that reaches the far row (the opponent's back row) is crowned a king, shown with a crown.
  • Kings move and capture both forward and backward, still one square at a time (no flying kings).
  • If a piece is crowned during a jump, the turn ends immediately — it may not continue jumping as a king on that same turn.

Winning & losing

  • You win when black has no pieces left, or black starts a turn with no legal move (all remaining pieces blocked).
  • You lose when red has no pieces left, or you start a turn with no legal move.
  • Wins and losses in a scored game are recorded in Your Record on this device: total wins, total losses, current win streak, and best win streak.
  • Practice rounds are never recorded — no win, loss, or streak change, however the round ends.

Practice round

  • Pick Practice round above the board to start one; pick Scored game to go back. Switching either way starts a fresh round.
  • Same board and same rules — captures and multi-jumps are still mandatory, so you practise the real game.
  • The computer plays gently (Easy style) whatever the Difficulty setting says, and Difficulty is paused until you return to a scored game.
  • No score and no timer. Coach tips appear above the board on your turns.
  • Hint lights up a good move in green: the piece to move and the square to land on. Following it is optional — and if a capture chain continues, Hint works for the next jump too.
  • Undo stays available after a practice round ends, so you can take back the final move and try a different finish.

This version

  • Difficulty: Easy — the computer plays casually and sometimes at random. Medium — it looks 4 moves ahead. Hard — it looks 6 moves ahead.
  • Undo takes back your last move segment (each jump in a multi-jump is one segment) and is unavailable after the game ends.
  • There is no move timer and no scoring beyond win/loss — games are untimed and played for the win.
